Paper 2. Generative Operators in Language-Model Ensembles: What They Add to Their Starting Point and What They Cost

The second paper of the series: self-consistency, debate, answer synthesis and task decomposition, each measured against its own starting point and at the same cost in dollars, on a preregistered set of 200 problems. What Is Hard for LLMs in Our Problem Sets: Interim Results

An interim lab log of looking for problems current models cannot solve. Among the 37 hardest items of a 52–67-model public matrix about a quarter are confirmed benchmark defects (24%, at most 38%), and they show themselves: solving them is unrelated to model strength. Recent 2025–26 olympiads, AIMO and Reasoning Gym, and Project Euler with Python (not a reasoning test), fall to a Haiku 5.5 → Opus 5.5 cascade except for one wrong gold and one missing format rule, and a 100-step synthetic deduction is solved without error, bounded by output length rather than logic. · doi

Paper 1. Selection Operators in Language-Model Ensembles: How Much of the Gap to the Oracle They Collect

The first paper of the series split out of the working draft, as one clean study: voting, routing and judge panels over a pool of language models, measured by the share of the gap between the best member and the oracle they collect, on 941 re-audited problems, public correctness matrices and the SWE-bench leaderboard. Voting and routing collect nothing over a best member chosen on held-out data; where operators do beat the best member, the gain is usually insurance against a member that gives no answer. Draft Paper (v10). What an Unreliable Verifier Collects

A selector with an external check is the one operator that can reach the oracle on agent tasks, and this note measures what an imperfect check collects: a formula with false-accept and false-reject rates per task, checked on a released critic (R2E-Gym, 500 SWE-bench Verified bugs) and on pre-registered runs of a Claude Haiku judge and two test writers over the same 1,600 Qwen3-Coder candidates. Model verifiers err by rejecting correct patches on hard tasks; model-written tests accept 39–69% of wrong patches even after reading the code. Tests as a gate before a ranking judge collect 0.28 of the prize at eight candidates. · read · full text, doi

The ten notes assembled into one working paper with one yardstick: for each aggregation operator, voting, repeated sampling, debate, synthesis, judges, routing, decomposition, how much of the distance from the best member to the oracle it collects and when. Agents with Tools Fail Together

175 software agents, each a model wrapped in its own tools and workflow, scored on the same 500 SWE-bench Verified bugs: the seven best carry 1.4 independent votes, their majority ties the best of them, and only a perfect selector would gain ten points. One scaffold over 41 models and one model over 11 scaffolds give the same shape. On τ-bench logs one agent rerun four times is worth 1.4–1.9 runs, and four runs of the best model cover as much as four models once. Pre-registered, zero inference, public results only. doi

11 October 2026

Agents Break Together on Misuse, Apart on Injection

Five computer-use agents from four vendors on 150 OS-Harm tasks, pre-registered, at zero cost. When the user asks for harm, the agents that comply are mostly the same ones (tetrachoric ρ 0.67, about 1.4 effective votes of five); when a web page asks for harm, which agent obeys is close to idiosyncratic (ρ 0.28, no task breaks all five). On the same tasks safety breaches are more shared across models than task failures. doi

10 October 2026

Decomposition Buys Budget at Best, Nothing Usually

Cut a problem into steps and let the same model solve them one at a time, at the cost of one whole attempt. What it could not solve whole it still cannot: on the hard numeric problems none is gained in eight tries. The gain lands only on the problems where a single pass is cut off by the token cap, and in dollars a split attempt costs three to nine whole draws, so the single model ties or wins at equal spend. Three other models solving the parts cost more and solve no more. Pre-registered, 560 runs, $5.73. code · doi

8 October 2026

Where the Swarm Pays

One corpus of answer traces, seven reasoning models from five families, 240 hard problems, every cheap way of multiplying agents. The first finding is about the benchmarks: on the problems where all seven models were scored wrong, the gold label, the question or the grader was at fault on 45 of 48, and the "co-failure ceiling" on the problems the models finish is 0.998–1.000. The second is about counting: two of the models hit a token cap on the hard problems, and a cut-off answer scored as a wrong vote had manufactured most of debate's gain. Inside the box, plurality is below the best model on every layer; debate's gain is mostly a second chance for an agent that ran out of tokens, it gains one item on the problems every agent answered, and it ends unanimously wrong on four problems whose labels are unchecked; about half of a homogeneous debate's gain is re-reading one's own answer; personas add no measurable independence; synthesis recovers nothing. On verification the swarm pays a little: five judges picking among candidates lead the solvers' plurality by a margin that depends on the tie rule and is within noise, their strict majority is right 94% of the time where it exists, and peer disagreement flags errors at AUROC 0.98 against 0.93 for fifteen self-resamples. Asked to certify a single candidate they are solvers again, trustworthy only when unanimous. Revised three times the same day, after a label audit, a truncation audit and a reading of the code against the text, with every version kept and comparable; measured, not pre-registered; data and code released. code · doi

Debate Is Not Sampling

The modal ceiling of test-time scaling says plurality voting cannot beat the rate at which the most common answer is right. On 9,600 public debate traces, three-agent refinement beats the round-0 modal-hit rate on every weak model and corrects 31–45% of problems where all three agents started wrong; the exchangeability premise is what fails. The correlation ceiling does transfer: inter-agent correlation rises every round and the panel is one effective voter by round 3; stopping at unanimity beats the n_eff-plateau rule. Pre-registered, zero inference cost. code · doi

The Control Ate the Signal

Kim 2026 finds that diversity metrics barely predict majority-vote gain once accuracy is controlled for. On four public correctness matrices a heterogeneous Condorcet model with no dependence term predicts the same gain at Spearman 0.80 against 0.20 for the residualized metric; the tetrachoric dependence estimate adds 0.01. Gain over the best member is a nonlinear function of the accuracies that a linear control throws away. Pre-registered, zero inference cost. code · doi

There Is No Free Lunch After All

Notes before writing down a theorem. The boundary of what a model can solve is the closure of its training set under composition of bounded depth; swarms, chains, debate and short RLVR move mass inside it, only external data extends it. The claim, why it is nearly a tautology, where the content is, what threatens it, and the plan to test it. Nothing measured yet. doi
