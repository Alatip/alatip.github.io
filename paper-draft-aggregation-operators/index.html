Paper Draft (v8). Exploring the Boundaries of Multi-Agent Ensembles: An Evaluation of Aggregation Operators

The ten technical notes on this site, assembled into one paper with one yardstick: for every aggregation operator, how much of the distance between the best single member and the oracle it collects, on which data, and at what price. A working draft, not peer reviewed; the PDF is the deliverable.

12 October 2026draft v815 sections, 20 tables, 87 referencesdownload PDFdoi:10.5281/zenodo.23311676
Abstract An ensemble of language models can be no better than the share of problems that at least one of its members solves. Every way of combining members, which we call an aggregation operator, collects some part of the distance between the best single member and that ceiling. The paper measures that share, on the same data and with the same yardstick, for plurality voting, repeated sampling of one model, multi-round debate, synthesis, selection and certification by judges, routing by an orchestrator, and decomposition into subtasks. The data are a text corpus of 733 problems answered by seven models from five vendors, with every jointly failed problem re-audited; the same corpus with the frontier models removed; 9,600 public debate traces; the SWE-bench Verified leaderboard matrix of 175 agent submissions, at the top and in mid-ranked accuracy bands; two public leaderboard matrices; released τ-bench and OS-Harm logs; and a cost-matched study of one model's draws. Operators that only rearrange answers already produced collect nothing, whether the room above the best member is 2 points or 32, because the members fail on the same hard problems: one latent difficulty factor reproduces their joint failures and their votes, and the independent Condorcet model over-predicts the vote by up to 27 points. Operators that add computation collect a few points under conditions the paper names: repeated sampling where a single draw is below 0.9 at about ten times one answer's tokens; debate where the members' errors are unshared, at a loss of coverage; a judge panel whose sign against the best member depends on the tie rule. The one large prize nobody collects is the oracle on agent tasks, 10 to 32 points above the best submission, which a selector with an external verifier might reach.

What the draft is built from

Status and what is still open

This is a working draft: the results are the notes' results, re-read with the controls a referee would ask for (held-out best member, pre-registered runs with their missed predictions reported, tie rules and in-sample choices marked). It has not been peer reviewed. Known open items, listed in the paper's Section 13: the text corpus is saturated and four of its models ran under an 8,192-token cap; the label audit was one-sided; several tie rules were chosen in sample; the agent matrices are leaderboard submissions; the partial-verifier measurement on agent tasks has not been made. Volume and page details of the six classical references are to be verified before any submission.
